## 2026-07-03 - Nikon support response regarding terminology definition

### Inquiry

The manual contains the following statement:
> Throughout this document, the display in the camera monitor and viewfinder during shooting is referred to as the “shooting display”.

Regarding this statement, a request was made to Nikon to provide detailed internal and manual-specific definitions, as well as concrete examples, for the terms "during shooting" / 「撮影時」 and "shooting display" / 「撮影画面」.

### Relevant response
After checking with the relevant department, Nikon stated that no publicly available definition could be provided for the relevant terminology.

This creates a significant terminology problem. The camera is physically performing continuous shooting, yet the displayed state is classified as not “during shooting” and not a “shooting display.” The classification therefore appears to depend not on whether shooting is actually in progress, but on which display state is currently active.

## 2026-11-01 - Summary of Q01–Q09: Cross-Case Findings

Following the inquiries under specific conditions, additional inquiries Q01 through Q09 were made. The main findings obtained from these questions are summarized below.

These questions were not intended to define Nikon’s internal firmware logic. Rather, they were intended to clarify how Nikon Support classifies observable display states under specific, reproducible operating conditions.

A key result is that the terms “during shooting,” “shooting display,” “Info display,” “Live View,” and “not applicable” do not form a simple hierarchy. In particular, Nikon Support’s responses show that:

- Display 5 / Info can be included in the custom monitor shooting-display system, while still being classified as neither “during shooting” nor a “shooting display.”
- A state may contain a real-time through-the-lens image and still not be classified as a “shooting display” when certain setting-adjustment overlays are present.
- Conversely, Display 5 / Info contains no through-the-lens image, yet it can be retained or restored as the active display state before, during, or after shooting-related operations.
- The camera can physically record images while the LCD state before and after exposure is classified as neither “during shooting” nor a “shooting display.”




### Q01–Q09 Overview

| Question | Main topic | Main finding |
|---|---|---|
| Q01 | Relationship between “during shooting” and “shooting display” | Nikon Support indicated that, outside exposure, playback, and menu states, states classified as “during shooting” normally have a “shooting display.” However, later answers introduced exceptions and qualifications. |
| Q02 | Display 5 / Info | Display 5 / Info is treated as the Info display controlled by the custom monitor shooting-display system, but it is not classified as “during shooting” and is not classified as a “shooting display.” |
| Q03 | Live View, shooting display, and i-menu | Live View and shooting display are closely related in simple shooting-display states, but once the i-menu is overlaid, the whole display is no longer classified simply as a shooting display. |
| Q04–Q06 | i-menu display states | When the i-menu is displayed over Live View or Info, Nikon Support classified the whole state as not belonging to the ordinary categories such as shooting display, Info display, or i-menu screen in the simple sense. Operationally, it is treated as “a state in which the i-menu is displayed.” |
| Q07 | Live View, through-image, and shooting display | Nikon Support distinguished between the real-time through-the-lens image itself and the classification of the whole display. A through-image can be present while the whole display is not classified as a shooting display. |
| Q08 | Display 1 and temporary shooting overlays | Some overlays, such as level display or exposure-compensation display, remain within the shooting-display classification. Other setting-adjustment overlays, such as white-balance or metering-mode adjustment, can make the whole display “not applicable,” even though the through-image remains visible. |
| Q09 | Manual-focus lens, focus peaking, and white-balance overlay | Even when a through-image, focus peaking, manual focusing capability, and shutter release are available, Nikon Support stated that a white-balance adjustment display can make the state not qualify as “a state in which the subject can be viewed while shooting,” while still classifying it as “during shooting.” In addition, they replied that "a state in which the subject can be viewed while shooting" means "a state in which the subject is visible as the main." |


### Main Pattern

Across Q01–Q09, the most important pattern is not merely that some states are classified differently. The important point is that the classifications do not map cleanly onto the photographer’s task state.

For a photographer, the practical questions are:

- Is the subject visible?
- Can composition be confirmed?
- Can focus be confirmed?
- Can the shutter be released?
- Does half-press return the camera to a subject-visible shooting state?

Nikon Support’s classifications do not always follow these task-oriented criteria. For example, Display 5 / Info is not classified as “during shooting” or as a “shooting display,” yet it can remain active before and after exposure and can be restored as the LCD display state. Conversely, some states with a visible through-image and usable focusing aids may still be classified as not being a “shooting display” when a setting-adjustment overlay is present.


### Relation to the Observed Scenes

The case-by-case scenes above illustrate these classifications in operational form.

In SCENE 0.0, Nikon Support explicitly classified Display 5 / Info as neither “during shooting” nor a “shooting display,” while also confirming that it is the Display 5 / Info screen included in the custom monitor shooting-display system.

In SCENE 2.2, Display 5 / Info can still appear even when Display 5 is disabled in d19. However, under that setting, opening the LCD monitor returns the camera to Live View Display 1. This suggests that disabling Display 5 does not eliminate the Info display as a screen type; rather, it changes whether that Info state is retained as the LCD-open display target.

In SCENE 3, the camera is physically performing continuous shooting, yet the LCD state can be classified as neither “during shooting” nor a “shooting display” when Display 5 / Info is active.

In SCENE 4, Display 5 / Info can replace the LCD composition-support display during a multiple-exposure sequence, even though the EVF correctly shows the through-the-lens view with the previous exposure superimposed.

In SCENE 5.2, a photograph can be taken from a Display 5 / Info state that Nikon classifies as neither “during shooting” nor a “shooting display.” After exposure, the camera returns to Display 5 / Info, while the recorded file is nevertheless classified as a captured image.


### Interpretation

These answers suggest that the central issue is not simply whether Display 5 / Info is called a “shooting display.” Nikon Support repeatedly classified Display 5 / Info as not being a shooting display.

The operational issue is different: Display 5 / Info, despite having no through-the-lens image and not being classified as a shooting display, can still behave as a retained or restored display state in contexts where the photographer expects to return to a subject-visible shooting state.

In other words, the problem lies in the mismatch between:

- the display classification used in support responses;
- the retained display state used by the camera;
- and the photographer’s task expectation that half-press, LCD opening, EVF return, or post-exposure recovery should restore a composition-capable display.

This mismatch is the basis for the Display 5 / Info persistence problem discussed in this report.
